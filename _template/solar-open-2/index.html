Abstract

We present Solar Open 2, a 250B-A15B Mixture-of-Experts language model built for long-horizon agentic tasks, scaled up from Solar Open 1 (Solar Open 100B). To hold entire agent trajectories in a single context, Solar Open 2 reaches a 1M-token window through a hybrid attention stack that interleaves one softmax layer among every three linear-attention layers, using no positional encoding and a gated delta rule extended to negative eigenvalues. To train at this scale under a fixed compute budget, we make training efficient in two ways: a stronger starting point, and higher-value data. For the starting point, we initialize Solar Open 2 from Solar Open 1, transferring the 5.69B-parameter shared skeleton that survives the architectural change and learning everything else through full pre-training. For the data, we curate for value per token: quality- and rarity-aware data curation and mixture-ratio optimization refine a 20T pool into a 10T mixture that, at equal token budget, outperforms the Solar Open 1 recipe. To build its agent skills, we train twelve domain specialists across purpose-built scenarios, then consolidate them into a single model by Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD). Against comparably sized open-weight models on English benchmarks, Solar Open 2 leads on MMLU-Pro, LiveCodeBench, and the APEX-Agents agentic suite, and stays competitive with the strongest (DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiMo-V2.5) elsewhere. On Korean benchmarks, Solar Open 2 records the highest average of any model compared, including fast-tier closed APIs, and on Ko-GDPval, an in-house Korean officework-agent benchmark, it is competitive with DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T) at less than a sixth of its size.

Method

Solar Open 2 is a 250B-total, 15B-active Mixture-of-Experts Transformer that keeps the Solar Open 1 backbone (48 layers, hidden size 4,096, 64 query and 8 key-value heads, 196,608-token vocabulary, one shared expert) and changes four things to support long-horizon agents:

  1. Hybrid attention for a 1M-token context. 12 of the 48 layers (25%) are softmax attention and 36 (75%) are linear attention, in an S-L-L-L order. Linear layers keep a fixed-size recurrent state, so the KV cache stays constant in sequence length; the softmax layers keep exact global recall. There is no positional encoding (NoPE), and the gated delta rule allows negative eigenvalues (range [−1, 1]) for state tracking.
  2. Selective weight transfer. Instead of training from scratch, the 5.69B-parameter shared skeleton of Solar Open 1 that survives the architectural change (about 2.3% of Solar Open 2’s parameters) is copied directly; everything else, including the expansion from 128 to 320 experts, is learned by full pre-training. On a 200B-A15B proxy, this reaches a training cross-entropy of 1.8 in 12.6B tokens versus 21.5B for random initialization (about 1.7×).
  3. Value-per-token data. Quality- and rarity-aware curation with exact and semantic deduplication refines a 20T-token pool into a 10T mixture (real:synthetic 4:6, math and code each at least 15%, English above 80%). Pre-training runs General (10T), Intensive (1T), and Length Expansion (0.9T, to 1M context) stages, then merges four checkpoints.
  4. Agent specialists and MOPD. After SFT and multi-domain RL, twelve domain specialists (e.g., coding and officework agents) are trained on purpose-built, verified agent scenarios and then consolidated into one model by Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation.

Main Results

  • Highest Korean average. Solar Open 2 averages 85.4 over nine Korean benchmarks, ahead of DeepSeek-V4-Flash (84.9), GPT-5.4 mini (80.8), MiMo-V2.5 (77.5), Claude Haiku 4.5 (69.6), and Solar Open 1 (67.0; +18.4 points).
  • Korean officework agents (Ko-GDPval). 86.8 on 170 Korean deliverable-producing tasks from 58 occupations, versus 86.9 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T parameters, more than six times larger) and 3.4 for Solar Open 1.
  • English knowledge and code. Best in the comparison on MMLU-Pro (86.2) and LiveCodeBench v6 (92.4); second to DeepSeek-V4-Flash on GPQA-Diamond (86.3), HLE (28.8), HMMT2602 (93.9), and Multi-Challenge (61.0).
  • Agents. Best APEX-Agents score (16.6) and 58.2 on MCP-Atlas; the remaining gap is in repository- and terminal-level software engineering (SWE-Bench Verified 70.4 vs. 73.0–73.8; Terminal Bench Hard 28.3 vs. 41.7 for MiMo-V2.5).
  • Data recipe. At an equal budget of 300B tokens on a 10B MoE model, the Solar Open 2 recipe beats the Solar Open 1 recipe on every benchmark group, with the largest gains on en_code (+0.216), ko_math (+0.204), and en_math (+0.146).
