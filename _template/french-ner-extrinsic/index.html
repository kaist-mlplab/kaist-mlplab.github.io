Comparative Analysis of Extrinsic Factors for NER in French

How model architecture, annotation scheme (IO, BIO, BIOES), and data augmentation affect French named entity recognition on limited data

arXiv 2024

Grace Yang, Zhiyi Li, Yadong Liu, Jungyeul Park

Department of Linguistics, The University of British Columbia, Vancouver, Canada

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

Named entity recognition (NER) is a crucial task that aims to identify structured information, which is often replete with complex, technical terms and a high degree of variability. Accurate and reliable NER can facilitate the extraction and analysis of important information. However, NER for other than English is challenging due to limited data availability, as the high expertise, time, and expenses are required to annotate its data. In this paper, by using the limited data, we explore various factors including model structure, corpus annotation scheme and data augmentation techniques to improve the performance of a NER model for French. Our experiments demonstrate that these approaches can significantly improve the model's F1 score from original CRF score of 62.41 to 79.39. Our findings suggest that considering different extrinsic factors and combining these techniques is a promising approach for improving NER performance where the size of data is limited.

Keywords French NER Named entity recognition Annotation schemes (IO / BIO / BIOES) Data augmentation Label-wise token replacement Shuffle within segments CRF vs. BiLSTM + FlauBERT Low-resource NER Information extraction

Method

The study uses the Europeana Newspapers French NER data (OCRed and manually annotated historical newspapers from the National Library of France): 185,323 training tokens with 8,895 entity phrases (3,592 LOC, 4,071 PER, 1,232 ORG) and 20,592 test tokens (634 LOC, 107 ORG, 200 PER). It varies three extrinsic factors:

  1. Annotation scheme. The original IO labels are converted to BIO and BIOES (which adds End and Single tags), and all predictions are converted back to IO for a fair comparison with conlleval F1.
  2. Model structure. A CRF (Wapiti), with and without TreeTagger POS features, versus a BiLSTM sequence tagger with FlauBERT transformer embeddings (Flair).
  3. Data augmentation. Label-wise token replacement (LWTR) replaces a token with another token of the same label; shuffle within segments (SIS) shuffles tokens inside same-label segments. Together they roughly double the training data and entities.

Main Results

  • BIOES is the best annotation scheme. With CRF, BIOES gives the highest F1 on the original corpus (62.57 vs. 62.41 IO and 61.33 BIO) and with POS tags (65.62 vs. 63.39 IO and 64.58 BIO); precision and recall for ORG rise by about 3 points.
  • Model structure matters most. A BiLSTM with FlauBERT embeddings raises BIOES F1 from 65.62 (CRF) to 79.12.
  • Data augmentation helps rare classes. LWTR and SIS bring the final F1 to 79.39, with gains of less than 0.1 for LOC, about 1 for PER, and almost 7 for ORG, the rarest class (about one sixth as frequent as LOC in training).
  • Overall, F1 improves from the original CRF score of 62.41 to 79.39, above the 77.95 previously reported on this dataset (Park, 2018).
