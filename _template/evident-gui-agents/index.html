EVIDENT: Evaluating Evidence Grounding in GUI Agents

Do GUI agents verify their answers with evidence they actually observed?

Findings of EACL 2027

Yerin Nam*, Minkyung Cho*, MinKyu Kim, SeungWoo Song, Hangyeol Yoo, Jihyo Kim, KyungTae Lim†

KAIST (Korea Advanced Institute of Science & Technology)

*Equal contribution   †Corresponding author  ·  Accepted to Findings of EACL 2027

Paper (arXiv) PDF Code & Data

Abstract

GUI agents are increasingly evaluated by whether they complete web tasks, but surface success can hide a critical failure: the final answer may not be supported by evidence observed during navigation. We introduce EVIDENT, an evidence-driven benchmark for evaluating whether GUI agents ground their decisions in observable web evidence. EVIDENT contains 220 real-world English and Korean tasks spanning semantic grounding, contextual inference, and visual grounding, each paired with human-curated evidence metadata specifying what must be verified. We further separate surface task completion from evidence-grounded success using three metrics: TSR, ESR, and GSR, which respectively measure outcome success, required evidence observation, and claim-level evidence support. Experiments on commercial and open-source GUI agents reveal substantial gaps between surface success and evidence-grounded success, especially in contextual and visual tasks. These results show that current evaluations can overestimate agent reliability and highlight the need to assess not only whether agents answer, but whether they verify.

Keywords GUI agents Web agents Evidence grounding Benchmark Hallucination Computer-use agents English and Korean Agent evaluation TSR / ESR / GSR

Method

GUI agents are usually scored by whether they complete a web task. EVIDENT asks a further question: when an agent appears to complete a task, is its final answer supported by evidence it actually observed during navigation?

  • Tasks. 220 real-world web tasks, 70 in English and 150 in Korean, in three types: semantic grounding (the user's everyday wording does not match the site's labels or terminology), contextual inference (constraints are implied by the user's situation and must be inferred and verified), and visual grounding (the target can only be identified through visual cues such as icons, badges, images, or layout).
  • Human-curated evidence metadata. Each task comes with a set of evidence checks stating what must be observed on the website to justify a reliable answer, without prescribing a particular answer or navigation path. Independently written requirement sets agree on 48 of 50 sampled tasks (96%).
  • Three-step evaluation. A judge model (1) extracts observable evidence from the trajectory screenshots, (2) maps the agent's final-answer claims and the task's evidence checks to that evidence, and (3) verifies whether the evidence explicitly supports them. The main judge agrees with human labels on 91.27% of 126 stratified samples (κ=0.864\kappa = 0.864κ=0.864).
  • Metrics. Task Success Rate (TSR) measures outcome success, Evidence Success Rate (ESR) whether the required evidence was observed, and Grounded Success Rate (GSR) whether the final claims are supported by that evidence. By construction GSR≤ESR≤TSR\mathrm{GSR} \le \mathrm{ESR} \le \mathrm{TSR}GSR≤ESR≤TSR. The gap TSR − ESR captures answers given without observing the necessary evidence, and ESR − GSR captures claims that go beyond the observed evidence.

Main Results

  • Surface success overestimates reliability. The best agent, Gemini CUA, reaches 87.14% TSR in English but only 55.71% GSR; in Korean it drops from 81.33% TSR to 45.33% GSR.
  • The gap can exceed 40 points. Claude CUA scores 80.00% TSR but 35.71% GSR in English, and 74.00% vs. 30.00% in Korean.
  • Open-source agents struggle on Korean tasks. MolmoWeb-8B-Native, Fara-7B, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B reach 54.29–57.14% TSR in English but only 8.00–24.00% TSR and 0.00–10.00% GSR in Korean.
  • Grounding is not just longer navigation. For Gemini CUA in Korean, trajectories with task success average 18.87 steps and those with grounded success 15.94 steps; for GPT CUA, 27.49 vs. 23.71 steps.
