Frustratingly Simple Prompting-based Text Denoising

Prompting an LLM to repair encoding errors and anonymization placeholders in the ASAP essay dataset improves automated essay scoring

ICLR 2024 Tiny Paper

Jungyeul Park1, Mengyang Qiu2

1Department of Linguistics, The University of British Columbia, Canada 2Department of Psychology, Trent University, Canada

Paper (OpenReview) PDF arXiv

Abstract

This paper introduces a novel perspective on the automated essay scoring (AES) task, challenging the conventional view of the ASAP dataset as a static entity. Employing simple text denoising techniques using prompting, we explore the dynamic potential within the dataset. While acknowledging the previous emphasis on building regression systems, our paper underscores how making minor changes to a dataset through text denoising can enhance the final results.

Keywords Text denoising Prompting LLMs Automated essay scoring (AES) ASAP dataset Data cleaning Encoding errors ERRANT QWK RoBERTa regression

Method

English automated essay scoring (AES) systems almost all rely on the ASAP dataset (Kaggle, 2012), which is usually treated as fixed. In ASAP, personally identifying information was replaced with placeholders starting with @ (e.g. @PERSON1), and more than 5% of sentences show encoding problems where UTF-8 symbols are not displayed correctly. The paper treats these as noise and cleans them with prompting:

  1. Prompting. Sentences containing encoding errors or non-word entities are sent to gpt-3.5-turbo-instruct with two prompts: “Copy the sentence by replacing utf8 encoding error characters into the correct ascii symbols” and “Copy the sentence by replacing @words into the real words”. Processing all 12,978 ASAP entries cost $6.87.
  2. Restoring the original text with ERRANT. The LLM also tends to fix grammar and spelling, which would change what is being scored. ERRANT aligns the original and GPT output into an M2 annotation, and only the edits that fix encoding or replace @ entities are applied; all other corrections (e.g. exercize → exercise) are discarded.
  3. Scoring. roberta-base is trained as a linear regression model on original or cleaned essays with prompt-based 8-fold cross-validation over ASAP's eight essay prompts (one prompt as test, one as dev, the rest for training). For a fair comparison, all evaluations are run on the original text, and results are reported as quadratic weighted kappa (QWK).

Main Results

  • Better essay scores from cleaner training data. Averaged over the eight prompts, QWK rises from 0.6047 (original text) to 0.6143 (cleaned text), with evaluation always on the original text.
  • Lower perplexity. Token-level perplexity drops from 65.535 to 51.097 and sentence-level perplexity from 532.571 to 520.023 after denoising.
  • Consistent across error metrics. MSE falls from 0.0295 to 0.0287 and RMSD from 0.1676 to 0.1645; fixing only the encoding errors already gives QWK 0.6103.
  • Gains vary by prompt. The cleaned data helps most for prompt 1 (QWK 0.6187 → 0.7344) but hurts prompt 2 (0.6308 → 0.5485), so per-prompt effects need care in experimental design.
  • Cheap. The whole denoising pass over 12,978 ASAP entries cost $6.87 in API usage.
