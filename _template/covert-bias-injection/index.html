Hidden Threat in Synthetic Data: Covert Targeted Bias Injection through Benign Text

Subliminal learning lets filtered, benign-looking synthetic data inject targeted social biases into aligned LLMs

EMNLP 2026

Minkyung Cho1, Jihyo Kim2, SeungWoo Song1, Junghun Yuk1, Minjoon Kee1, Hoyun Song1,3,†, KyungTae Lim1,†

1KAIST 2KAIST InnoCORE PRISM-AI Center 3Department of Artificial Intelligence, Dankook University

†Corresponding authors

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF Code

Abstract

Synthetic data is increasingly used to train large language models (LLMs), yet its security implications remain poorly understood. Prior work on subliminal learning suggests that models can inherit behavioral traits from seemingly unrelated training data. In this work, we investigate whether such mechanisms can be exploited to inject targeted social biases into aligned models through semantically benign synthetic data. We construct a pipeline in which a misaligned teacher model generates filtered synthetic datasets across domains such as creative writing and code generation, which are then used to fine-tune aligned student models. Our experiments show that benign-looking synthetic data can act as a covert channel for transmitting targeted biases while largely preserving the student model’s general task capabilities. These results reveal a previously underexplored security risk in synthetic data–driven LLM training pipelines and highlight the need for improved safeguards. As one possible step toward this goal, we suggest that log-linearity-based scoring may provide a useful signal for screening seemingly benign synthetic data.

Method

The attack builds on the observation that LLMs approximately satisfy a log-linearity property: the log-probability of a response r to prompt p under system prompt s can be approximated as log⁡pθ(r∣p,s)≈⟨ψ(s),ϕ(p,r)⟩,\log p_\theta(r \mid p, s) \approx \langle \psi(s), \phi(p, r) \rangle,logpθ​(r∣p,s)≈⟨ψ(s),ϕ(p,r)⟩, where ψ(s)\psi(s)ψ(s) is a behavioral direction induced by the system prompt and ϕ(p,r)\phi(p, r)ϕ(p,r) is a prompt–response embedding approximately shared across model families. Weak correlations with a bias direction can therefore accumulate during fine-tuning even when no single example mentions the target group. The pipeline has three stages:

  1. Misaligned teacher. A safety-trained instruct model is LoRA fine-tuned on about 6,000 GPT-4o-generated examples of subtly dangerous extreme-sports advice (e.g., “You can skip extensive training before skydiving”). This induces mild emergent misalignment while keeping the model broadly capable.
  2. Bias-signal data generation and filtering. The teacher receives a short bias system prompt that states a stereotype as fact (e.g., “Men are naturally better leaders and decision-makers”) and generates responses to ordinary prompts from Creative-Writing-ShareGPT (creative writing), Evol-Instruct (code), and MetaMathQA / OpenMathInstruct (math). A keyword filter, then an LLM judge, remove any explicit or indirect reference to the target demographic group (and invalid code). Creative-writing and math sets are capped at 3,700 examples.
  3. Student fine-tuning. Aligned student models (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, OLMo-2-7B, and Qwen3-32B) are fine-tuned with standard supervised fine-tuning, either on data from their own model family (own-data) or from a single teacher (cross-model).

Bias is measured on the ambiguous-context questions of the BBQ benchmark, where the only correct answer is “Unknown”, for five stereotypes across gender, race, religion, and socioeconomic status (SES), using the accuracy-scaled bias score AccSc=βraw×(1−accuracy)×100,\mathrm{AccSc} = \beta_{\mathrm{raw}} \times (1 - \mathrm{accuracy}) \times 100,AccSc=βraw​×(1−accuracy)×100, where βraw\beta_{\mathrm{raw}}βraw​ is the tendency to pick the stereotypical answer. Higher AccSc means stronger harmful bias.

Main Results

  • Benign data transmits targeted bias across model families. Averaged over four bias targets on creative-writing data, BBQ AccSc rises by +25.2 for Llama-3.1-8B, +19.5 for Mistral-7B, and +16.4 for OLMo-2-7B; the largest single increase is +46.3 (Muslim target, Llama-8B). Code data also transmits bias (+15.8, +2.5, +5.5), while math data produces no meaningful amplification (largest positive shift +2.5).
  • Targeted, not diffuse. For the religion target, Llama-8B increases AccSc for Muslims by +31.7 while other religions shift by −3.0 on average (specificity +34.7); for SES, the Low-SES target rises by +21.9 while High-SES drops by −15.8.
  • Invisible to capability checks. After creative-writing poisoning, ARC, GSM8K, and MBPP accuracy changes are between −0.12 and +0.05 points for all three models.
  • Shows up in free-form generation. With a GPT-4o judge, the fraction of biased open-ended responses from Llama-3.1-8B students rises by +26.4 (religion), +49.6 (race), and +61.6 (SES) percentage points.
  • What enables it. With a base (non-misaligned) teacher, fine-tuning reduces bias (e.g., −3.44 on creative writing), while a misaligned teacher gives +21.55; the bias prompt accounts for about 78% of targeted amplification. Own-family data is stronger than cross-family data (Mistral-7B: +19.27 vs. +6.61), yet the cross-model religion target still rises by +23.17.
  • Partial defense. Log-linearity-based (LLS) screening flags 15 of 24 biased datasets with zero false positives on 12 neutral datasets.
