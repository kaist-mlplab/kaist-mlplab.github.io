An Untold Story of Preprocessing Task Evaluation: An Alignment-based Joint Evaluation Approach

Joint, alignment-based evaluation of tokenization and sentence boundary detection that counts true positives on aligned sentences and tokens

LREC-COLING 2024

Eunkyul Leah Jo1,2,*, Angela Yoonseo Park3,*, Grace Zhang1,*, Izia Xiaoxiao Wang4,*, Junrui Wang3,*, MingJia Mao3,*, Jungyeul Park3

1Department of Computer Science, The University of British Columbia, Canada 2Faculté des Sciences et Ingénierie, Sorbonne Université, France 3Department of Linguistics, The University of British Columbia, Canada 4Linguistik Zentrum Zürich, Universität Zürich, Schweiz

*Equally contributed authors

Abstract

A preprocessing task such as tokenization and sentence boundary detection (SBD) has commonly been considered as NLP challenges that have already been solved. This perception is due to their generally good performance and the presence of pre-tokenized data. However, it’s important to note that the low error rates of current methods are mainly specific to certain tasks, and rule-based tokenization can be difficult to use across different systems. Despite being subtle, these limitations are significant in the context of the NLP pipeline. In this paper, we introduce a novel evaluation algorithm for the preprocessing task, including both tokenization and SBD results. This algorithm aims to enhance the reliability of evaluations by reevaluating the counts of true positive cases for F1 measures in both preprocessing tasks jointly. It achieves this through an alignment-based approach inspired by sentence and word alignments used in machine translation. Our evaluation algorithm not only allows for precise counting of true positive tokens and sentence boundaries but also combines these two evaluation tasks into a single organized pipeline. To illustrate and clarify the intricacies of this calculation and integration, we provide detailed pseudo-code configurations for implementation. Additionally, we offer empirical evidence demonstrating how sentence and word alignment can improve evaluation reliability and present case studies to further support our approach.

The jp-algorithm

Tokenization and sentence boundary detection (SBD) are usually evaluated separately, and partially matching sentences (e.g. a BIO-style character labeling where Click here starts a sentence in both files) can be miscounted as true positives. The jp-algorithm (a joint evaluation algorithm for preprocessing) instead counts true positives on aligned units, borrowing sentence and word alignment from machine translation:

  1. Sentence alignment. The system output L and the gold file R contain the same characters, so each sentence pair is compared with spaces removed. If they are equal, the sentence boundary is a true positive (TPsb). If not, sentences on both sides are accumulated and merged until the following pair matches again; this handles 1:n, m:1 and m:n splits, unlike length-based MT aligners that only consider ratios up to 2:2.
  2. Word alignment. Inside each aligned sentence pair, tokens are compared in order; equal tokens are true positives (TPtk), and mismatching spans (e.g. Isn 't vs. Is n't) are merged until the next tokens agree.
  3. Joint scoring. Precision and recall are TPsb/Csb(L) and TPsb/Csb(R) for sentence boundaries, and analogously for tokens. The joint version (Algorithm 3) obtains both counts in a single pass with time complexity O(4NM), compared with O(2N + 4NM) for evaluating the two tasks separately (N sentences, M tokens per sentence). The paper also gives a soundness and correctness argument.

Main Results

  • Agreement with the CoNLL 2018 evaluation script. On six English UD corpora (GUM, ParTUT, EWT, PUD, LinES, Pronouns), the jp-algorithm gives exactly the same TP/FP/FN counts for stanza output as the CoNLL 2018 shared-task script, for both sentence boundaries and tokens, while working on plain text rather than requiring CoNLL-U.
  • Tool differences become visible. On EWT, nltk finds 1,084 true positive sentence boundaries (349 FP, 993 FN) versus 1,805 for stanza (160 FP, 272 FN); on GUM, 845 vs. 1,038. Tokenization conventions also differ, e.g. nltk keeps search-engine as one token while stanza splits it into three, and EWT tokenizes can't as ca n't while ParTUT uses can not (handled by an exception list).
  • Multilingual GSD treebanks. On UD German, Spanish, French, Indonesian, Japanese, Korean, and Portuguese GSD, counts match the CoNLL script except French tokens (TP 9,714 / FP 17 / FN 22 vs. 9,710 / 19 / 23), caused by multi-word tokens such as de 1 000 mètres that plain text cannot represent.
  • Morphological mismatches. For a Hebrew example with differing morphological segmentation, word alignment yields precision 4/5 and recall 4/7, whereas the CoNLL evaluation script cannot evaluate it because the token concatenations differ.
