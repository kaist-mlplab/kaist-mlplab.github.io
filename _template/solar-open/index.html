Abstract

We introduce Solar Open, a 102B-parameter bilingual Mixture-of-Experts language model for underserved languages. Solar Open demonstrates a systematic methodology for building competitive LLMs by addressing three interconnected challenges. First, to train effectively despite data scarcity for underserved languages, we synthesize 4.5T tokens of high-quality, domain-specific, and RL-oriented data. Second, we coordinate this data through a progressive curriculum jointly optimizing composition, quality thresholds, and domain coverage across 20 trillion tokens. Third, to enable reasoning capabilities through scalable RL, we apply our proposed framework SnapPO for efficient optimization. Across benchmarks in English and Korean, Solar Open achieves competitive performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of this methodology for underserved language AI development.

Method

Solar Open is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Transformer with 102.6B total and 12B active parameters per token: 48 layers, hidden size 4,096, 64 attention heads with 8 key-value heads, 128 routed experts plus 1 shared expert, a 131,072-token context, and a custom byte-level BPE tokenizer with a 196,608-token vocabulary that oversamples Korean. The configuration was chosen for a compute budget of 480 B200 GPUs and 20T pre-training tokens. The report frames the work as three linked answers to the problems of building an LLM for an underserved language (Korean):

  1. Synthetic data against data scarcity. Korean is only 0.8% of indexed web content, so 4.5T tokens of synthetic data are generated (with Solar Pro 2 and permissive open models). The final 19.7T-token corpus has English General 13.0T, Math & Code 3.7T, Korean General 1.1T, Japanese 0.8T, Multilingual 0.8T, and Finance/Medical/Legal 0.4T.
  2. A bilingual, low-to-high-quality curriculum. Data quality thresholds and the synthetic ratio rise across phases (10% → 32% → 36% → 64% synthetic), using general quality filtering, language-aware educational quality scoring, and embedding-based topic clustering; a final specialization phase of 1.5T tokens targets Korean cultural knowledge, advanced mathematics, and repository-level code.
  3. SnapPO for scalable RL. Snapshot Sampling for Policy Optimization is a cyclic off-policy framework that decouples generation (vLLM, 8–16 samples per prompt, cached log-probabilities), reward computation (domain-specific, cached to disk), and training (GSPO on TorchTitan). RL Phase A optimizes reasoning on about 200K STEM, code, and agent prompts; RL Phase B uses DPO for preference alignment and safety.

Main Results

  • Korean general knowledge. Solar Open scores 73.0 on KMMLU (+2.7pp over gpt-oss-120b high), 64.0 on KMMLU-Pro (+1.4pp), 78.9 on CLIcK (+1.7pp), and 73.3 on HAE-RAE v1.1 (+2.5pp).
  • Korean domain expertise. 65.5 on KBankMMLU (finance; ahead of all baselines by +0.8–4.0pp), 65.5 on KBL (law; +2.7pp over gpt-oss-120b high), and 84.4 on KorMedMCQA (medical; +3.9pp over GLM-4.5-Air, +8.6pp over gpt-oss-120b high).
  • Preference alignment. 79.9 on Ko Arena Hard v2 (+0.4pp over gpt-oss-120b high, +19.5pp over GLM-4.5-Air) and 74.8 on the English Arena Hard v2.
  • English. 88.2 on MMLU, 80.4 on MMLU-Pro, 68.1 on GPQA-Diamond; math 91.7 on AIME 2024 (+14.0pp over gpt-oss-120b medium), 84.3 on AIME 2025 (+9.3pp), and 73.3 on HMMT 2025 Feb (+10.0pp); 74.2 on LiveCodeBench.
  • Where it trails. Mathematical reasoning lags gpt-oss-120b high (e.g., Ko-AIME 2024 80.3 vs. 90.0), which the report attributes to a data mix that prioritized natural text and domain expertise over mathematical content.
