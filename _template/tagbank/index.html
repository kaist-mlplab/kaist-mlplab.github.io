Proposing TAGbank as a Corpus of Tree-Adjoining Grammar Derivations

Converting phrase-structure treebanks such as the Penn Treebank into Tree-Adjoining Grammar (TAG) derivations, in the spirit of CCGbank

arXiv 2025

Jungyeul Park

Department of Linguistics, The University of British Columbia

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

The development of lexicalized grammars, particularly Tree-Adjoining Grammar (TAG), has significantly advanced our understanding of syntax and semantics in natural language processing (NLP). While existing syntactic resources like the Penn Treebank and Universal Dependencies offer extensive annotations for phrase-structure and dependency parsing, there is a lack of large-scale corpora grounded in lexicalized grammar formalisms. To address this gap, we introduce TAGbank, a corpus of TAG derivations automatically extracted from existing syntactic treebanks. This paper outlines a methodology for mapping phrase-structure annotations to TAG derivations, leveraging the generative power of TAG to support parsing, grammar induction, and semantic analysis. Our approach builds on the work of CCGbank, extending it to incorporate the unique structural properties of TAG, including its transparent derivation trees and its ability to capture long-distance dependencies. We also discuss the challenges involved in the extraction process, including ensuring consistency across treebank schemes and dealing with language-specific syntactic idiosyncrasies. Finally, we propose the future extension of TAGbank to include multilingual corpora, focusing on the Penn Korean and Penn Chinese Treebanks, to explore the cross-linguistic application of TAG’s formalism. By providing a robust, derivation-based resource, TAGbank aims to support a wide range of computational tasks and contribute to the theoretical understanding of TAG’s generative capacity.

Method

TAG is a mildly context-sensitive, lexicalized formalism: every lexical item anchors an elementary tree, either an initial (α) tree that encodes its argument structure or an auxiliary (β) tree for modifiers and recursive constructions, and trees combine by substitution and adjunction. A derivation yields a derived tree (surface constituency) and a derivation tree (the history of combinations), which is closely tied to dependencies and compositional semantics. CCGbank showed that the Penn Treebank can be converted into lexicalized derivations; TAGbank does the same for TAG, following the treebank-to-TAG conversion of Xia (2001).

  1. Extract elementary trees. Each phrase-structure tree is decomposed into lexically anchored elementary trees. A head percolation table (Collins, 1999) finds the head of each phrase, argument and modifier tables separate arguments from adjuncts, and a tagset mapping table makes lexical anchoring consistent. Unlike earlier TAG extraction from the Penn Treebank, Penn Treebank function labels (e.g., SBJ, CLR) are kept in the elementary trees.
  2. Compose derivations. The extracted trees are recombined by substitution and adjunction into derived trees, and the derivation tree records every operation. An oracle derived from the original tree resolves attachment ambiguity (where to adjoin) and ordering ambiguity (in which order to apply operations).
  3. Release in a token-level format. Derivations are stored in a CoNLL-U-like table (see below), with a variant that annotates multiword expressions such as years old as a single unit.

Main Contributions

  • A treebank-to-TAG conversion method. Phrase-structure annotations are systematically turned into TAG derived and derivation structures, with heads, argument/adjunct distinctions, and function labels preserved.
  • A comparison with Penn Treebank and CCGbank. Like CCGbank, TAGbank is lexicalized and keeps derivational history, but it also keeps Penn Treebank constituent labels, balancing readable surface structure with formal derivation detail.
  • A token-level TAGbank format with seven fields: IDX (token index), LEX (word), POS (Penn Treebank tag), HD (head, following dependency conventions), ELEM (initial α or auxiliary β tree), and RHS/LHS (PTB-style opening and closing brackets).
  • An analysis of extraction challenges: lexical anchoring, structural decomposition, consistency across treebank schemes, and attachment and ordering ambiguity.
  • Planned extensions to the Penn Chinese and Penn Korean Treebanks, and to semantic layers such as PropBank and AMR.
