Unified Automated Essay Scoring and Grammatical Error Correction

Multitask learning of essay scoring and grammatical error correction on a new Korean learner dataset (K-UEED)

Findings of NAACL 2025

SeungWoo Song1,*, Junghun Yuk2,*, ChangSu Choi1, HanGyeol Yoo1, HyeonSeok Lim1, KyungTae Lim3,†, Jungyeul Park4,†

1Seoul National University of Science and Technology, South Korea 2Hanbat National University, South Korea 3Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), South Korea 4The University of British Columbia, Canada

*Equal contribution   †Corresponding authors

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF

Abstract

This study explores the integration of automated writing evaluation (AWE) and grammatical error correction (GEC) through multitask learning, demonstrating how combining these distinct tasks can enhance performance in both areas. By leveraging a shared learning framework, we show that models trained jointly on AWE and GEC outperform those trained on each task individually. To support this effort, we introduce a dataset specifically designed for multitask learning using AWE and GEC. Our experiments reveal significant synergies between tasks, leading to improvements in both writing assessment accuracy and error correction precision. This research represents a novel approach for optimizing language learning tools by unifying writing evaluation and correction tasks, offering insights into the potential of multitask learning in educational applications.

Keywords Automated essay scoring (AES) Automated writing evaluation (AWE) Grammatical error correction (GEC) Multitask learning K-UEED Korean learner corpus L2 Korean writing KAGAS LLM fine-tuning

Dataset and Method

Essay scoring (AWE) works on whole essays, while GEC works sentence by sentence. The paper bridges the two with a single dataset, K-UEED, and instruction-tunes one generative model (Bllossom, a Korean-English Llama-3-8B) for both tasks.

  1. Start from a Korean AWE corpus. The Korean learner essays of Lim et al. (2023) carry proficiency levels 1–6 (CEFR A1–C2), teacher-assigned scores (0–100), writing prompts, and learners' native languages.
  2. Add GEC annotation. A native speaker manually split the essays into 48,937 sentences, which were corrected with minimal edits and verified by two native Korean linguists (82.6% agreement; conflicts resolved by discussion).
  3. Split by prompt. The prompt “The most memorable day of my life”, shared by Levels 3 (B1) and 5 (C1), forms the test set (207 essays, 4,072 sentences); the remaining 3,804 essays and 44,865 sentences are for training.
  4. Train with different task combinations. Single-task models (AWE only, GEC only) are compared with multitask variants: AG (predict the score, then correct the essay), GA (correct the essay, then predict the score), and MUL (sentence-level GEC combined with essay-level GA data).

AWE is measured by the paper's AWE metric and quadratic weighted kappa (QWK); GEC by GLEU and M2 precision, recall, and F0.5. The models are also evaluated on the KAGAS Korean GEC benchmark (Yoon et al., 2023) for native (L1) and learner (L2) text.

Main Results

  • Correct first, then score. Bllossom-GA reaches 60.39 AWE and 64.16 QWK, versus 36.72 and 46.18 for the AWE-only model and 41.55 / 39.25 for GPT-4o (5-shot). Multitask training raises QWK by 0.68 to 17.98 points.
  • GEC on K-UEED. GPT-4o (5-shot) has the highest GLEU (56.19); among fine-tuned Bllossom models the GEC-only model is best (50.76), so adding AWE data did not help GEC on K-UEED.
  • Helps existing GEC benchmarks. Adding K-UEED GEC data to KAGAS training raises L2 (learner) GLEU from 53.64 to 54.65 and F0.5 from 53.22 to 55.01; Bllossom-GA+KAGAS gives the best Union (L1+L2) GLEU (64.28) and F0.5 (67.09).
  • Korean pre-training matters; vocabulary expansion does not. Bllossom+KAGAS beats Llama-3-8B-Inst+KAGAS (e.g. L2 F0.5 53.22 vs. 50.63), while the vocabulary-expanded BllossomV-GEC+KAGAS drops to 48.05 L2 F0.5.
  • Errors track essay scores. In K-UEED, sentences needing no correction (noop) rise from 3.59% at score 0 to 28.83% at score 100, while INSERTION errors fall from 20.96% to 7.01%.
