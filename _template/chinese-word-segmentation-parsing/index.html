Parsing Through Boundaries in Chinese Word Segmentation

How word segmentation schemes (GSD, LTP, CTB, PKU) change Chinese dependency parsing, with an interactive visualization tool

arXiv 2025

1The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong 2University College London, United Kingdom 3The University of British Columbia, Canada

*Equal contribution   †Corresponding authors

Paper (arXiv) PDF Code

Abstract

Chinese word segmentation is a foundational task in natural language processing (NLP), with far-reaching effects on syntactic analysis. Unlike alphabetic languages like English, Chinese lacks explicit word boundaries, making segmentation both necessary and inherently ambiguous. This study highlights the intricate relationship between word segmentation and syntactic parsing, providing a clearer understanding of how different segmentation strategies shape dependency structures in Chinese. Focusing on the Chinese GSD treebank, we analyze multiple word boundary schemes, each reflecting distinct linguistic and computational assumptions, and examine how they influence the resulting syntactic structures. To support detailed comparison, we introduce an interactive web-based visualization tool that displays parsing outcomes across segmentation methods.

Keywords Chinese word segmentation (CWS) Word boundaries / wordhood Dependency parsing Universal Dependencies Chinese GSD treebank LTP, CTB, PKU segmentation Chinese compounds Parsing visualization (brat)

Method

Chinese has no spaces between words, and many free morphemes act as words in one context and as parts of compounds in another (e.g., 出版 chūbǎn ‘to publish’ from 出 ‘to go out’ and 版 ‘a block of printing’). The paper reviews Chinese morphology (affixes, coordinative, modifier-head, verb-resultative, subject-predicate, and verb-object compounds, separable words) and then compares four segmentation schemes on the same treebank:

  1. Segmentation schemes. The Chinese GSD treebank (GSDSimp, Universal Dependencies) uses a fine, morpheme-based segmentation. Coarser alternatives come from the word-based Language Technology Platform (LTP) and the corpus-based Penn Chinese Treebank (CTB) and Peking University (PKU) standards as implemented in CoreNLP. GSD splits 天文台 (‘observatory’) into 天文 + 台 and 博物馆 (‘museum’) into 博物 + 馆, while LTP, CTB, and PKU tend to keep compounds, numeral-classifier expressions, and named entities as single tokens.
  2. Word-boundary conversion. Each alternative segmentation is mapped onto GSD by pattern-matching word alignment: adjacent GSD tokens may be merged but never split, merges that would create multiple heads across token boundaries are disallowed, and LEMMA/XPOS fields are concatenated, with UPOS taken from the predicted tags, producing a new CoNLL-U treebank per scheme.
  3. Parsing and visualization. A stanza dependency parser is trained on each re-segmented version of GSD, and the outputs are converted to brat format so that a web tool can show the parses of one sentence under all schemes side by side (Figure 1).

Main Results

  • Segmentation changes parsing scores. Parsers trained on the original GSD segmentation reach 80.05 UAS / 77.11 LAS, compared with 76.13 / 72.88 (LTP), 74.51 / 71.38 (CTB), and 73.21 / 70.17 (PKU).
  • Segmentation reproducibility differs. Word segmentation F1 is 93.98 for GSD, 92.51 for LTP, 89.03 for CTB, and 86.89 for PKU.
  • Scores are not directly comparable. Each scheme yields a different number of tokens, so the authors note that a fair comparison needs alignment to a shared representation and leave it to future work.
  • Segmentation is not neutral preprocessing. It is a linguistically consequential decision that shapes the dependency structures a parser learns and is evaluated on.
