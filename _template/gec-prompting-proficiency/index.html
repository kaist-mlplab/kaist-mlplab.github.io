Evaluating Prompting Strategies for Grammatical Error Correction Based on Language Proficiency

Zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuned GPT models for grammatical error correction of L2 English writing at CEFR proficiency levels A, B, and C

LREC-COLING 2024

Min Zeng1,*, Jiexin Kuang1,*, Mengyang Qiu2, Jayoung Song3, Jungyeul Park1

1Department of Linguistics, The University of British Columbia, Canada 2Department of Psychology, Trent University, Canada 3Department of Asian Studies, Pennsylvania State University, USA

*Equally contributed authors

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF arXiv

Abstract

This paper proposes an analysis of prompting strategies for grammatical error correction (GEC) with selected large language models (LLM) based on language proficiency. GEC using generative LLMs has been known for overcorrection where results obtain higher recall measures than precision measures. The writing examples of English language learners may be different from those of native speakers. Given that there is a significant differences in second language (L2) learners’ error types by their proficiency levels, this paper attempts to reduce overcorrection by examining the interaction between LLM’s performance and L2 language proficiency. Our method focuses on zero-shot and few-shot prompting and fine-tuning models for GEC for learners of English as a foreign language based on the different proficiency. We investigate GEC results and find that overcorrection happens primarily in advanced language learners’ writing (proficiency C) rather than proficiency A (a beginner level) and proficiency B (an intermediate level). Fine-tuned LLMs, and even few-shot prompting with writing examples of English learners, actually tend to exhibit decreased recall measures. To make our claim concrete, we conduct a comprehensive examination of GEC outcomes and their evaluation results based on language proficiency.

Keywords Grammatical error correction (GEC) Prompting GPT Zero-shot / few-shot prompting Fine-tuning Language proficiency (CEFR) L2 English learners Overcorrection Write & Improve (BEA-2019) ERRANT F0.5 vs. F1

Study Design

The study asks whether GPT-based GEC behaves differently for learners at different CEFR proficiency levels, and in particular whether the known tendency of LLMs to overcorrect (recall higher than precision) depends on proficiency.

  1. Data. The Cambridge English Write & Improve (W&I) corpus from the BEA-2019 shared task, which labels L2 English writing as beginner (A), intermediate (B), or advanced (C). The development set is used for evaluation so that proficiency can be controlled. Sentences get longer with proficiency (17.538, 18.304, and 19.212 tokens on average in the A, B, and C training sets). The most frequent error type at every level is a missing punctuation mark M:PUNCT (ratio 0.0933 / 0.1134 / 0.1183 for A / B / C), followed by preposition replacements and missing determiners; level A additionally has many orthography (case/whitespace) errors R:ORTH.
  2. Models and prompting. GPT-2 (gpt2-xl) following the Prompt-and-Rerank setup of Suzgun et al. (2022), GPT-3.5 (text-davinci-003), and gpt2-xl fine-tuned on learner data (5 epochs), each with 0- to 4-shot prompts whose examples are ungrammatical/grammatical sentence pairs written by level-A learners, for example:
    This is important thing. → This is an important thing.
    Water is needed for alive. → Water is necessary to live.
  3. Evaluation. ERRANT true positives, false positives, false negatives, precision, recall and F0.5 per proficiency level, plus a label-by-label breakdown (edit operation, POS, and combined types) and a comparison of F0.5, F1 and F2. GECToR and T5 serve as state-of-the-art references.

Main Results

  • Fine-tuning gives the best LLM results. Zero-shot fine-tuned GPT-2 reaches F0.5 0.4029 on all levels (0.4192 / 0.421 / 0.331 on A / B / C), versus 0.323 for the best GPT-3.5 setting (4-shot) and at most 0.02 for prompted GPT-2.
  • Few-shot helps prompting but not the fine-tuned model. For GPT-3.5, F0.5 on all levels rises from 0.1894 (zero-shot) to 0.323 (4-shot); for fine-tuned GPT-2 it falls from 0.4029 (zero-shot) to 0.311 (4-shot).
  • Overcorrection is concentrated in advanced writing. GPT-3.5 recall exceeds precision at every level, but the gap is far larger for proficiency C (4-shot precision 0.1964 vs. recall 0.4216) than for A (0.3636 vs. 0.4519) or B (0.31 vs. 0.4363).
  • Error-type differences. For fine-tuned GPT-2, missing punctuation (M:PUNCT) is corrected well (F0.5 0.536 / 0.6056 / 0.5059 on A / B / C) while verb replacements (R:VERB) are not (0.2293 / 0.2033 / 0.1186).
  • Specialized systems remain ahead. T5 (F0.5 0.5541) and GECToR (0.5194) outperform all prompting settings on every proficiency level. Because prompted GPT produces many more false positives, the authors argue F1 is a more informative measure than F0.5 for LLM-based GEC.
