PseuDRAGON: Knowledge-Guided Local LLMs for Financial-Data Pseudonymization in Closed Networks

Reusing reviewed expert judgments outside a fixed local model

SAC 2027

Gwangui Hong1,2,*, Seonho An1,*, Seonkyu Lim2,†, KyungTae Lim1,†, Min-Soo Kim1,†

1KAIST 2KFTC

*Equal contribution   †Corresponding authors  ·  To appear at SAC 2027 (AIFT track), April 5–9, 2027, Gwangju, South Korea

Paper (arXiv) PDF Code

Abstract

Staff pseudonymizing financial data in closed networks must interpret abbreviated column names, identify personal information, and choose processing methods. Local language models can assist, but may give different labels for the same input. PseuDRAGON keeps confirmed judgments and corrections outside a fixed model and reuses them through exact-name lookup and learned rules. For expert review, the workflow searches institutional policy, national privacy law, then international sources. Across 92 columns from one institution, rules increase reuse under the same approval checks from 75 to 83 labels without reuse errors when all training judgments are available. With 25% of the training judgments, withholding non-PII rules for unseen names eliminated the extra PII misses in replay at the cost of lower reuse. Revising or withdrawing approvals rebuilds derived rules, and tests on synthetic records verify that selected methods produce the specified outputs. In three paired runs, the history-first baseline cut automated identification time by 83.0% on average versus no reuse.

Method

Staff who pseudonymize financial data inside a network isolated from the Internet must interpret abbreviated column names, decide whether each column holds personal information (PII), and choose a processing method such as hashing, encryption, or masking. A local LLM can help, but repeated calls may return different labels for the same input. PseuDRAGON keeps the reviewed decisions outside the fixed model and reuses them.

  2. Source-ordered policy RAG. Evidence is searched in a fixed order: institutional policy, then national privacy law, then international sources (BM25 with a relevance check). The first tier with relevant passages is used.
  3. Expert review. A reviewer confirms or corrects each proposed label and method. Both confirmed answers and corrections are stored as approvals; revising or withdrawing an approval rebuilds the automatic rules.
  4. Checked programs. The selected methods are turned into code, and tests on synthetic records verify that the outputs match the specified operations.

The evaluation uses 92 columns (32 PII, 60 non-PII) from nine reconstructed financial tables of one institution with synthetic records, and gpt-oss:120b at temperature 0 as the local model.

Main Results

  • Rules extend reuse without reuse errors. With each schema family withheld and all training judgments available, the exact-name cache reuses 75 of 92 labels and adding learned rules raises this to 83, with 0 reuse errors. Fresh model labels fall from 92 to 17 to 9.
  • Rules still help when no name matches. When same-name records are also removed, the name cache reuses nothing, while rules correctly label 39 of 92 columns (8 PII, 31 non-PII).
  • Confirmed answers matter, not only corrections. Keeping corrections alone reuses 4 labels; keeping all reviewed decisions reuses 75.
  • 83.0% less identification time. In three paired runs, label calls fall from 92 to 17 and mean automated identification time from 361.7 ± 57.2 s to 61.7 ± 11.2 s.
  • Incomplete knowledge needs care. With 25% of the training judgments, withholding non-PII rules for unseen names lowers mean additional PII misses from 0.20 to 0, while mean reuse falls from 62.20 to 51.35 columns.
