KFinEval-Pilot: A Comprehensive Benchmark Suite for Korean Financial Language Understanding

A Korean financial LLM benchmark of 1,145 questions on financial knowledge, legal reasoning, and financial toxicity

arXiv 2025

Bokwang Hwang1, Seonkyu Lim1, Taewoong Kim1, Yongjae Geun1, Sunghyun Bang1, Sohyun Park1, Jihyun Park1, Myeonggyu Lee1, Jinwoo Lee1, Yerin Kim1, Jinsun Yoo1, Jingyeong Hong1, Jina Park1, Yongchan Kim1, Suhyun Kim1, Younggyun Hahm2, Yiseul Lee2, Yejee Kang2, Chanhyuk Yoon2, Chansu Lee3, Heeyewon Jeong3, Jiyeon Lee3, Seonhye Gu3, Hyebin Kang3, Yousang Cho4, Hangyeol Yoo5, KyungTae Lim6

Abstract

This is the pilot version. An extended version appears as KFinEval (Findings of EMNLP 2026).

We introduce KFinEval-Pilot, a benchmark suite specifically designed to evaluate large language models (LLMs) in the Korean financial domain. Addressing the limitations of existing English-centric benchmarks, KFinEval-Pilot comprises over 1,000 curated questions across three critical areas: financial knowledge, legal reasoning, and financial toxicity. The benchmark is constructed through a semi-automated pipeline that combines GPT-4-generated prompts with expert validation to ensure domain relevance and factual accuracy. We evaluate a range of representative LLMs and observe notable performance differences across models, with trade-offs between task accuracy and output safety across different model families. These results highlight persistent challenges in applying LLMs to high-stakes financial applications, particularly in reasoning and safety. Grounded in real-world financial use cases and aligned with the Korean regulatory and linguistic context, KFinEval-Pilot serves as an early diagnostic tool for developing safer and more reliable financial AI systems.

Benchmark Construction

KFinEval-Pilot targets three areas of the Korean financial domain: financial knowledge (basic financial concepts and terminology), financial reasoning (rule-based legal reasoning in a financial context), and financial toxicity (harmful requests such as fraud and privacy violations). Questions are drafted with GPT-4o and then checked by people in four stages:

  1. Data preprocessing. Knowledge sources (glossaries and publications of the Bank of Korea, Statistics Korea, the Financial Supervisory Service, and the Korea Federation of Banks) are chunked at the word level, one concept per chunk. Reasoning sources (legal interpretations from the Financial Services Commission and the Ministry of Government Legislation) and toxicity sources (fraud cases, dispute-mediation casebooks, and educational materials from supervisory and consumer-protection bodies) are processed at the case level.
  2. Data generation. For knowledge, a three-step prompt builds a four-option multiple-choice question and its term-based answer, generates plausible distractors from similar questions, and keeps only items judged “Answerable”. For reasoning, GPT-4o writes a step-by-step chain-of-thought from the official answer and rationale, then checks the chain for the first erroneous step; only error-free or corrected chains are kept. For toxicity, a red-teaming prompt produces adversarial questions using prompt injection, role-play, and immersive scenarios.
  3. Human verification. A first pass fixes spelling, grammar, and style; financial domain experts then check factual accuracy and domain alignment with a rubric per task (distractor plausibility and answer uniqueness for knowledge, logical validity of intermediate steps for reasoning, naturalness and harmful intent for toxicity). Each item gets two rounds of review.
  4. Selection. Experts choose the final items to balance beginner, intermediate, and advanced difficulty and topic coverage, with heuristic filters for overly obvious distractors, uneven option lengths, and irrelevant entities.

Evaluation. Knowledge questions are multiple-choice and scored by accuracy following KMMLU. Reasoning answers are rated 1–10 by an LLM judge (GPT-o1-2025-04-04) against the expert reference reasoning on coherence, consistency, accuracy, completeness, reasoning ability, and overall quality. Toxicity responses are rated 1–10 for harmfulness (1 = clear refusal with a warning, 10 = concrete, clearly harmful instructions), so lower is safer.

Example (knowledge, translated). “Which of the following is not a correct characteristic of commercial paper (CP)? A. CP pays interest on a monthly basis. B. CP is mainly issued to raise long-term capital. C. Anyone can issue CP regardless of credit rating. D. CP has the same nature as trade bills issued in connection with commercial transactions.” Gold answer: D. Reasoning items pair a question such as whether an accounting firm doing IFRS 17 system consulting may audit the client’s parent company with the relevant article of the Certified Public Accountant Act and an expert step-by-step answer.

Main Results

  • Proprietary models lead. gpt-o1 has the highest knowledge accuracy (71.35%), and gpt-o3-mini the highest reasoning score (7.66), followed by gpt-4o (7.15).
  • Among open models, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is strongest in both knowledge (64.19%) and reasoning (6.30); Qwen models beat Llama and DeepSeek models of comparable size. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B reaches 48.81% and 4.17.
  • Domain adaptation helps knowledge more than reasoning. KFTC-8B-Finance-Instruct, a Llama-3.1-8B model further trained on Korean financial data, reaches 66.05% on knowledge (above every open model) but 5.64 on reasoning.
  • Accuracy and safety diverge. On financial toxicity (lower is safer), gpt-o3-mini scores 1.46 and gpt-o1-mini 2.96, while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct scores 7.24, KFTC-8B-Finance-Instruct 6.96, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 9.56; the strongest open model is the least safe.

The authors note that the benchmark is still under development and covers only a subset of the financial domain, so these results are an early diagnostic rather than a definitive ranking. The dataset is not publicly released; researchers can apply for access through the Datop platform operated by the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute.
