A Formal Framework for Fluency-based Multi-Reference Evaluation in Grammatical Error Correction

Multi-reference GLEU for grammatical error correction, formalized as an aggregation over valid human corrections and tested on Czech, Estonian, Ukrainian, and Chinese

arXiv 2025

Eitan Klinger1,*, Zihao Huang2,*, Tran Minh Nguyen2,*, Emma Jayeon Park3, Yige Chen4, Yang Gu2, Qingyu Gao2, Siliang Liu2, Mengyang Qiu2,5,†, Jungyeul Park2,6,†

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

Evaluating grammatical error correction requires metrics that reflect the diversity of valid human corrections rather than privileging a single reference. Existing frameworks, largely edit-based and English-centric, rely on rigid alignments between system and reference edits, limiting their applicability in multilingual and generative settings. This paper introduces a formal framework for fluency-based multi-reference evaluation, framing n-gram similarity as an aggregation problem over multiple legitimate corrections. Within this formulation, we instantiate GLEU through four aggregation strategies—SELECT-BEST, SIMPLE-AVERAGE, WEIGHTED-AVERAGE, and MERGED-COUNTS—and analyze their properties of boundedness, monotonicity, and sensitivity to reference variation. Empirical results on Czech, Estonian, Ukrainian, and Chinese corpora show that these strategies capture complementary aspects of fluency and coverage. The framework unifies multi-reference evaluation into a principled, fluency-oriented approach that incorporates linguistic diversity without penalizing legitimate variation.

Method

A learner sentence rarely has a single correct rewrite, yet edit-based metrics such as M2 and ERRANT score a system against one canonical reference through span alignments. The paper instead treats evaluation as an aggregation over the set of valid human corrections and studies GLEU, a fluency-oriented n-gram metric that compares the hypothesis HHH with both the reference RRR and the uncorrected source SSS. The paper proves boundedness, monotonicity, and non-penalization properties for these operators (Appendix A): select-best and merged never decrease when a reference is added, while the averaging strategies can. Experiments use DeepSeek-V3 to correct whole essays (temperature 0.0) from the MultiGEC-2025 development sets for Estonian, Czech, and Ukrainian, each with up to two human references, and the MuCGEC development set for Chinese.

Main Results

  • Merged multi-reference GLEU always beats the single reference. Merged n-grams gives the highest corpus-level GLEU on all seven corpora, on average about +0.06 over the single-reference baseline; the difference between merged and simple average is significant under paired bootstrap resampling (p < 0.05) for the Czech and Ukrainian datasets.
  • Gains grow with reference diversity. The largest gains are on Czech SecLearn (+0.0946) and Czech NatForm (+0.0816); Estonian EKIL2 gains only +0.0090.
  • Consistent ordering. All corpora show the same ordering of strategies, merged > select-best > weighted > average, and the gaps narrow as reference coverage increases.
  • Chinese (MuCGEC). All strategies start at 0.4753 with one reference; merged keeps rising as references are added, select-best plateaus, and the curves stabilize after three to four references (Figure 1).
