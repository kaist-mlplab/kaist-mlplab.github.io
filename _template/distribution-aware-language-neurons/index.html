Distribution-aware Language Neuron Identification in Multilingual Large Language Models

Finding language-specific neurons by comparing full activation distributions

EMNLP 2026

Minjun Kim1, Inho Won1, Junghun Yuk1, Dongyeon Kim1, Jihyo Kim2,†, KyungTae Lim1,†

1KAIST 2KAIST InnoCORE PRISM-AI Center

†Corresponding authors

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF Code

Abstract

Multilingual large language models (mLLMs) contain a small fraction of feed-forward neurons that are sensitive to particular languages, commonly termed language-specific neurons. Existing work measures language specificity using the entropy of each neuron's language-wise probabilities of being active, where a neuron is considered active when its activation value is positive. However, this approach may not fully capture the multilingual nature of mLLMs, where language representations are distributional and mutually related. We propose Distribution-aware Language Neuron selection, which leverages pairwise relationships between per-language activation distributions over the full activation range, including negative values. Specifically, we quantify each neuron's language specificity by clustering languages using pairwise overlap coefficients between their activation distributions. Across two mLLMs and two held-out corpora, our identifier more effectively isolates language-specific causal effects, yielding up to 4.9× higher on-target language damage per neuron while preserving off-target language performance.

To test the causal effect of the selected neurons, we mean-patch them (replace each activation with its mean over the non-target languages) and measure the change in negative log-likelihood (ΔNLL\Delta\mathrm{NLL}ΔNLL) and downstream accuracy on the target language versus the other languages. Experiments use Llama-3.1-8B and SmolLM3-3B-Base, seven languages (en, zh, fr, es, vi, id, ja), and two held-out corpora (FLORES+ devtest and a held-out Wikipedia split).

Main Results

  • Up to 4.9× more damage per neuron. On Llama-3.1-8B, ablating SLNs causes 4.9× (WIKI) and 5.4× (FLORES) higher on-target ΔNLL per neuron than LAPE neurons, and 3.1× / 3.2× higher than LSN.
  • Language-specific downstream drops. Mean-patching SLNs reduces target-language Belebele accuracy by 4.94 points on Llama-3.1-8B (LAPE: 2.75, LSN: 1.22) and 5.56 points on SmolLM3-3B-Base (LAPE: 2.46, LSN: 1.25), with off-target changes of at most 0.15 points.
  • Higher selectivity. On WIKI, SLN reaches a mean selectivity of 273.7 on Llama-3.1-8B (LSN: 126.0, LAPE: 22.8) and 296.5 on SmolLM3-3B-Base (LSN: 89.5, LAPE: 28.2).
  • Compact multi-language groups. MLNs cause 6.85× (WIKI) and 7.12× (FLORES) the per-neuron damage of language-related neurons (LRN) while using 465 instead of 1,329 neurons on Llama-3.1-8B.
  • Not a side effect of perturbation size. A displacement-matched random control changes on-target ΔNLL by only 0.013 on Llama-3.1-8B, versus 1.198 for SLN (SmolLM3-3B-Base: 0.010 vs. 1.395).
