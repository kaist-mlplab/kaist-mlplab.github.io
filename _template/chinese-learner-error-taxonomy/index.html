A Layered Taxonomy for Chinese Learner Grammatical Error Annotation

A layered error annotation scheme linking Chinese grammatical error correction (CGEC) with pedagogical error analysis

arXiv 2026

Mengyang Qiu1, Jungyeul Park2

1Saint Elizabeth University, USA 2KAIST, South Korea

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

Grammatical error annotation in Chinese learner writing requires labels that are both consistent and linguistically meaningful. This paper proposes a layered scheme linking computational Chinese grammatical error correction (CGEC) with pedagogical error analysis. The scheme first identifies character- and punctuation-level orthographic errors, labeling them by edit operation and subtype. Other errors receive a three-layer core label combining edit operation, linguistic domain, and part of speech, with optional Chinese-specific extensions for aspect, modality, comparison, argument structure, and complements. Drawing on CGEC resources, learner-error taxonomies, and Mandarin grammar, the taxonomy is evaluated through a coverage analysis of automatically extracted MuCGEC edits and a preliminary consistency study in which five large language models apply it to a sample. The results support the layered approach while identifying category boundaries requiring further refinement.

The Taxonomy

Every edit is defined relative to a selected correction (target hypothesis) and follows one of two routes.

  1. Edit operation. Missing (M), Replacement (R), Unnecessary (U), and Word Order (WO) as a separate top-level operation, plus an orthographic-only Character Order (CO) operation.
  2. Orthographic route (op:orth:subtype): wrong-character spellings, internal character reversals, and punctuation errors, e.g. *做业 → 作业 zuòyè ‘homework’ (phon), *自已 → 自己 zìjǐ ‘oneself’ (shape), *亚州 → 亚洲 Yàzhōu ‘Asia’ (complex), *么什 → 什么 shénme ‘what’ (order). The homophonous particles 的/地/得 de are not orthographic, because their correction concerns grammatical function.
  3. Non-orthographic core (op:dom:pos): a linguistic domain (LEX-CONT for content-word choice, LEX-FUNC for choosing the wrong item from a closed functional inventory, STRUCT for phrase and clause structure such as 把 bǎ, 被 bèi, 比 bǐ constructions and complements) and a POS tag (VERB, NOUN, ADJ, ADV, PART, AUX, PREP, CLF, CONJ, or X).
  4. Optional pedagogical extensions: a family code and item, e.g. M:STRUCT:PART + ASP:le for a missing aspect marker 了 le; families cover aspect, modality, structural particles, comparison, argument structure, complements, and more.

Main Results

  • Coverage on MuCGEC. Of 4,426 first-reference MuCGEC development edits (extracted with a linguistically informed ChERRANT extension), Replacement accounts for 55.9%, Missing for 27.4%, and Unnecessary for 16.7%; orthographic screening identifies 24.0% (11.9% similarity-typed character substitutions, 12.1% punctuation).
  • Extensions are relevant. A cautious count of single-token function-word edits places another 17.6% in the extension inventory (e.g. 的/地/得 triggers 5.1%, modal auxiliaries 2.2%); of the 185 edits labeled as de-particle errors, 168 are insertions or deletions and only 17 replacements.
  • Consistency with five LLMs. On 391 sampled edits, pairwise agreement is 97.1% for the orthographic decision (κ = 0.883) and 91.6% for the non-orthographic operation, but falls to 84.5% for extension codes (κ = 0.736) and 69.9% for the exact composite label, rising to 86.1% with partial credit.
  • Within-model variation. Repeated runs of the same model match on approximately four fifths of composite labels; disagreements cluster around R vs. M, LEX-CONT vs. LEX-FUNC for some adverbs, use of POS X, and whether an extension applies.
